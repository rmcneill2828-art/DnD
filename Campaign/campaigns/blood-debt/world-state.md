# World State — Blood Debt

_Living tracker — active threads only. Full history lives in `session-log.md`; this file should always be quick to scan._

## Party
- **Location:** At **the Drowned Hollow** ("Widow's Still"), the second of Kethrun's sealed sites, west of Urskelde past Corbin's holding. The seal is freshly reinforced and the site secured; the party is deciding whether to head back to Urskelde, on to one of the two remaining sealed sites (the Still Throat, north; a nameless valley, southeast near Threnn's village), or something else. Kaldur holds Urskelde as formally named commander, with Threnn as political adviser and Halvard running the spy network; Threnn himself is currently at the Hollow with his crew, not Urskelde.
- **House Corvane's betrayal, resolved (Session 24).** Lord Edrin Corvane publicly confessed to arranging the Ashpit hit as inherited duty to **the Raven Queen**, not personal malice. Offered redemption rather than vengeance, he stayed at Urskelde as a partner (see Key NPCs). The council pledged a full regional alliance against the threat above Malphestor.
- **Kethrun's three sealed sites, named (Session 25):** the Still Throat (north, frozen glacial pass), a nameless valley (southeast, near Threnn's village, Bettha may know more), and **the Drowned Hollow** (west, sealed in haste — chosen first for urgency). The old sensing-thread between sentinel sites broke down gradually over centuries, not all at once — Kethrun believes it could plausibly be rewoven site by site.
- **The Drowned Hollow, resolved (Session 25).** Found and calmed **Ilsevet**, an ancient goliath-kin who sealed the wound alone in haste centuries ago and had held it by herself ever since, believing herself forgotten. The site was drained via a rediscovered outflow channel, properly reinforced in a two-day ritual (Ysolde, Threnn's ritual-keepers, the bear-spirit anchoring it), and given sustainable tending — **Odra** (one of Threnn's masons) staying on to learn from Ilsevet directly, mirroring Torvald's arrangement with Kethrun.
- **The scout threat, active and confirmed real.** A bird-like, "built rather than born" creature — grafted, not fully alive — struck Darkhawk once near the Standing Ring and withdrew rather than press the fight; confirmed via Ysolde's Grave sense and Sael's tracking to be reporting back to something else via crude scrying, and to exist in more than one instance ("building them," not just one scout). A dampening shard was also found planted on one of Urskelde's own cairns — removed, but the backlash cost Ysolde a day's *Sending* and likely alerted whatever placed it.
- **Kaldur formally named Commander of Urskelde** in Darkhawk's absence, full authority to act, announced publicly at the gate. Threnn stands as his political adviser; Halvard's spy network reports to both.
- **The Standing Ring:** hidden stair beneath the central slab leads to **Kethrun**, an ancient goliath-kin-made stone sentinel guarding the original sealed wound. Seal freshly reinforced as of Session 18; Torvald stays on-site to tend it. Urskelde's own boundary cairns are confirmed as the same ancient ward-tradition, "lesser" in scale.
- **The ancient goliath-kin village:** Threnn's people (~3 dozen), released from a centuries-long protective freeze, have fully relocated to and settled at Urskelde.
- **Darkhawk and Mara are married** (Session 16) — see character sheets.
- **Duskgate is fully resolved** — anchor broken, bargain ended, market cleared. **The succession mechanic remains the live security concern:** the bargain needs a living successor to physically reach Duskgate to renew; Vale had groomed **Merrick** for this without telling him. Both are held under complete, tested wards at Urskelde. **Malphestor's fate is unknown** — purposeless, not hostile, hinted at something further up the chain that felt every banked soul vanish.
- **Thundertree losses at the entrance (Session 14):** three warriors dead (Ren Alderhawk, Bram and Torin Stonebrand). Yorik Stonebrand gave Rurik a mourning-stone; Ren's widow Vessa and daughter Kessa asked only that he be remembered as a friend.

## Active leads / evidence

## Player Choices of Consequence
A running pattern, not a fixed rule: Darkhawk has moved between ruthless (killing surrendering guards at the mine-road ambush, no-quarter at the fairground building) and merciful (turning the waystation and first relay stop rather than killing, freeing captives with a genuine choice to flee or fight, taking Vale alive) depending on the moment and who's in front of him — usually erring toward mercy when someone stops actively fighting, but not always. **The defining choice of the campaign so far, at Duskgate:** rather than kill Malphestor, Darkhawk engaged it honestly, learned the anchor (not the devil) was the real target, and risked his own soul to break it instead — leaving Malphestor alive and telling it "if anger can fall, maybe devils can rise." Full blow-by-blow of each encounter is in `session-log.md`.

## Loose Threads
- **Who betrayed Darkhawk at Ashpit — resolved (Session 24).** Lord Edrin Corvane confessed publicly: he arranged the hit as inherited family duty to **the Raven Queen**, not personal malice, but never held the blade himself. Parted from Darkhawk as partners, not friends.
  - **The actual killer:** a separately hired professional, working name possibly **"Greyfeather,"** vanished without a trace the same week Ashpit closed. Halvard's first assignment as spymaster.
  - **Unexplained:** the order naming Rurik as target came down unusually precise — cage number specified before the match was even scheduled. Corvane himself flagged this as worth taking seriously; unconfirmed why.
  - Still open: Rurik's original opponent's identity and fate — House Corvane's own attempt to trace him went cold decades ago.
  - Still open: how deep House Corvane's service to the Raven Queen runs, and whether it connects to what's above Malphestor. Corvane's own read: his goddess would likely see that thing as a thief in her domain, not a kindred force — unconfirmed, a hopeful theory rather than fact.
- **A possible pattern with the Raven Queen:** Ysolde's working theory is that her faith moves subtly, arranging rather than announcing — and Darkhawk's own Ashpit memory unlocking right before the council, after thirteen years blocked, fits that shape uncomfortably well. Unconfirmed, unprovable, but flagged.
- **"The count" — kept strictly between Darkhawk, Mara, Sael, and Ysolde, not yet shared with the wider household.** A dream-borne impression from the bear-spirit the night after the council: something vast and patient has been "counting" Darkhawk since before his memory begins — possibly since the raid that took him as a child, which killed two elders and a hunter and took exactly one child, not a general sweep. Working theory built out together: Ashpit's thirteen years may have been deliberate cultivation rather than cruelty, meant to ensure he'd finish a kill when it mattered; his refusal to do so may be the single most consequential choice of the war so far; Malphestor's broken ledger reflects him becoming genuinely unpredictable rather than just lucky; and the bear-spirit may be actively holding "the count" at zero — a true balance point — with every primal bond the party's formed since potentially part of the same ancient alliance Kethrun described, Darkhawk being prepared to eventually help hold that balance himself rather than just survive it. Deliberately not escalated — Darkhawk chose to prioritize verifiable evidence (Corvane's records, the temple's answer) over pure theory, and to bring this to Vosk, Threnn, Kaldur, and Kethrun directly only once there's something solid to act on, ideally at the Standing Ring where the bond runs strongest.
- **What's above Malphestor, confirmed to be actively searching for a new foothold** — testing multiple sealed points at once per both Kethrun's and Ilsevet's independent senses of it. Not yet found. Possibly connected to the Raven Queen thread via House Corvane, unconfirmed.
- **The scout creature(s)** — bird-like, "built rather than born," reports back via crude scrying circles, exists in more than one instance. Origin and maker unknown.
