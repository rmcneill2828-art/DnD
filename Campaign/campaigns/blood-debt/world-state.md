## DM tool: Travel Risk Tiers (adopted Session 44)
Roll 1d20 per travel leg; a result at or under the tier's threshold triggers an encounter, resolved on a sub-table built for that specific stretch of ground.
| Tier | Where it applies | Trigger | Flavor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Controlled** | Allied, patrolled ground (Urskelde ↔ Standing Ring, Kessick's Ford roads, etc.) | 1–2 | Mostly flavor even when triggered |
| **2 — Frontier** | Known but unpatrolled (past sealed sites, approach roads) | 1–5 | Real mix — nothing, or a genuine fight |
| **3 — Unknown/dangerous** | Unmapped, active-threat ground (toward the mountain keep) | 1–8 | Weighted toward real threats matching whatever's actually out there |

## DM tool: Camp Risk Tiers (adopted Session 44)
Same three tiers as travel, applied to overnight camps, with the trigger shifted by how well the camp was actually set:
| Prep level | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Well-prepared (watch rotation set, defensible site, wards/detection active) | **−2** to the tier's trigger |
| Adequate (basic watch, reasonable site, nothing special) | none |
| Poor (rushed, exposed, no real watch) | **+2** to the tier's trigger |

## Active leads / evidence
- **Kathrivex, the First Ledger** — the name behind "the count." Network confirmed far larger than previously scoped (hundreds of threads, Session 41); true source/location still unknown.
- **The fist-and-chain sigil hierarchy** — confirmed (Session 42, via the Auditor's remains) to sit above even the audits themselves. Same mark as Ravenshollow's contracts and the Warden's bargain. Scope unknown.
- **The map of other ledger sites — three prioritized by council (Session 43), first now reached (Session 44).** **1st: the mountain keep** — confirmed a construct foundry and network hub, occupied not destroyed; see Party section. Threads from its hub crystal appear to run toward the other two. **2nd: the poor coastal town** south of Halloway's Reach, where something is *held* rather than grown. **3rd: "the burned court"** inland — matches a legend in House Corvane's archive; the compass has never once pointed toward it. Neither yet visited.
- **"R." — first trace of whoever builds the network's infrastructure (Session 44).** Found in the mountain keep's study and personal quarters. Distinct maker's mark: a stylized eye ringed by a broken chain. Treats Kathrivex as a resource, not a master. A real person — evidence of people they love. Elenna (rescued from the keep) met R. twice: calm, patient, spoke once of unspecified greater "scale" being built toward beyond even the count. Halvard's archive may add more once he arrives.
- **A fourth site, previously unknown: "the Reckoning House."** Named in a transfer ledger recovered at the mountain keep, alongside confirmed entries for the burned court and the coastal town. Nothing else known — not location, not purpose.
- **The compass device** — recovered from Corlan's cabin, points toward network sites rather than north. Responsive but unreliable: flickers between the mountain keep and the poor coastal town, never the burned court. Under continued study by Ysolde and Wren.
- **The corrupted crystal — Weave-grown, not enchanted (Session 41).** Crystallized directly out of raw magic; implies trained, methodical craft behind the network's construction, not brute divine will.

## Player Choices of Consequence
A running pattern, not a fixed rule: Darkhawk has moved between ruthless (killing surrendering guards at the mine-road ambush, no-quarter at the fairground building) and merciful (turning the waystation and first relay stop rather than killing, freeing captives with a genuine choice to flee or fight, taking Vale alive) depending on the moment and who's in front of him — usually erring toward mercy when someone stops actively fighting, but not always. **The defining choice of the campaign so far, at Duskgate:** rather than kill Malphestor, Darkhawk engaged it honestly, learned the anchor (not the devil) was the real target, and risked his own soul to break it instead — leaving Malphestor alive and telling it "if anger can fall, maybe devils can rise."
