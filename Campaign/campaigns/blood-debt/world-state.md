## DM tool: Travel Risk Tiers (adopted Session 44)
Roll 1d20 per travel leg; a result at or under the tier's threshold triggers an encounter, resolved on a sub-table built for that specific stretch of ground.
| Tier | Where it applies | Trigger | Flavor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Controlled** | Allied, patrolled ground (Urskelde ↔ Standing Ring, Kessick's Ford roads, etc.) | 1–2 | Mostly flavor even when triggered |
| **2 — Frontier** | Known but unpatrolled (past sealed sites, approach roads) | 1–5 | Real mix — nothing, or a genuine fight |
| **3 — Unknown/dangerous** | Unmapped, active-threat ground (toward the mountain keep) | 1–8 | Weighted toward real threats matching whatever's actually out there |

## DM tool: Camp Risk Tiers (adopted Session 44)
Same three tiers as travel, applied to overnight camps, with the trigger shifted by how well the camp was actually set:
| Prep level | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Well-prepared (watch rotation set, defensible site, wards/detection active) | **−2** to the tier's trigger |
| Adequate (basic watch, reasonable site, nothing special) | none |
| Poor (rushed, exposed, no real watch) | **+2** to the tier's trigger |

## Session 51 (continued) — the plan against Kathrivex takes shape
- **The reframe: Voreth replaced Malphestor (Session 51).** Rurik's central insight, delivered directly to Voreth: Malphestor's original honest count had no opposing weight to check it, which is exactly the vacuum Kathrivex exploited totally — and Rurik himself is the anomaly that total imbalance produced. Voreth didn't invent the theft; he occupies the same role further from its origin, close enough to call it profit rather than crime. The fix isn't destroying one devil or crowning the other — it's **two honest, mutually-checking counts**, Voreth and Malphestor as equals building the practice together, with Kathrivex as the actual, sole target.
- **Voreth committed to meeting Malphestor directly, as equals (Session 51).** Visited the ward-ruin camp in person (a real gesture of trust, invited inside rather than met at range) and was genuinely moved by the "replaced Malphestor" framing. Left to set things in motion on his own side; Malphestor is traveling to the camp by his own choice to meet him.
- **The endgame theory: sever, don't fight (Session 51).** Ysolde's scholarship: Kathrivex's power is transactional (drawn from the tribute network itself), not faith-based — total, simultaneous severing at the true central hub would starve it back into a mere spirit rather than banish a god. Rurik's addition: gods rarely manifest physically by choice, bound by the same law the Raven Queen respects; forcing Kathrivex to intervene directly to defend its own domain would expose it to genuine vulnerability and to any god already holding a finding against it.
- **The real danger, named clearly (Session 51).** Severing the hub without Malphestor and Voreth both ready to simultaneously transfer every still-living thread network-wide risks a Debt Collapse at a scale far beyond anything faced so far. A siege against Kathrivex's avatar, if it manifests, will demand far more sustained endurance than the single duel with Voreth did — the whole party's resources planned around a long fight, not a decisive exchange.
- **Thossia's judgment, a second divine witness (Session 51).** Ysolde's Divine Intervention (now automatic at 20th level) put a direct question to her own goddess: does something standing in false judgment over mortals still owe a debt? Thossia answered: Kathrivex's authority is structurally illegitimate, a mortal spirit wearing a stolen office, and the debt is total, owed before the one it stole from. She named the target without naming a place — **"the seat where every debt not yet an anomaly still resolves"** — Kathrivex's true ledger, not the Reckoning House's infrastructure. Thossia's own office is the one Kathrivex has been directly counterfeiting, making her awareness more pointed than even the Raven Queen's.
- **Malphestor, en route to the ward-ruin camp (Session 51).** Reached by Sending, left Corren's Landing in Sennet's hands, traveling by his own choice to meet Voreth as an equal.
