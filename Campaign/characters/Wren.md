## Features & Traits

**Sorcerer**
- **Spellcasting:** Charisma-based. Spell save DC 19, spell attack +11. Knows spells (doesn't prepare) — 6 cantrips, 16 spells known. Slots: 4 (1st), 3 (2nd), 3 (3rd), 3 (4th), 2 (5th), 1 (6th), 1 (7th), 1 (8th, previously missing from this sheet — corrected Session 45), 1 (9th, new).
- **Font of Magic:** 19 sorcery points (equals sorcerer level, updated Session 51). Can convert sorcery points to spell slots and vice versa (Flexible Casting).
- **Metamagic (4 known):** *Quickened Spell* (2 pts — cast a normal-action spell as a bonus action), *Twinned Spell* (varies — target a second creature with a single-target spell), *Careful Spell* (1 pt — chosen creatures automatically succeed on the spell's save), **Sculpt Spell** (1 pt — added Session 48, RAW fix for the 4th option owed at 17th level; when casting a spell that affects other creatures in an area, up to 3 creatures of her choice automatically succeed on the save and take no damage if they would've taken half on a success — built for exactly the friendly-fire problem the party kept navigating around at the stone house).

**Wild Magic (Sorcerous Origin)**
- **Wild Magic Surge:** after casting a sorcerer spell of 1st level or higher, the DM can have Wren roll on the Wild Magic Surge table (1-in-20 chance per cast). Effects are random, often chaotic, occasionally spectacular — this is the whole point of her.
- **Tides of Chaos:** once per long rest (until triggered again), gain advantage on one attack roll, ability check, or saving throw. Using this gives the DM license to force a Wild Magic Surge the next time she casts a sorcerer spell.
- **Bend Luck** (6th level): reaction, spend 2 sorcery points to roll 1d4 and apply it as a bonus or penalty to another creature's attack roll, ability check, or saving throw.
- **Controlled Chaos** (14th level): when rolling on the Wild Magic Surge table, can roll twice and choose which result occurs.
- **Spell Bombardment** (18th level, Session 48/49): when a damage die rolls its highest possible number on a spell, reroll that die and add the new result to the damage too.

> **DM quick-check per sorcerer spell cast (1st level+): Wild Magic Surge uses a running counter, not flat 1-in-20 (house rule, Session 43 — fits her arc better). Surge threshold = 1 + consecutive non-surge casts since her last surge (0 misses = surge on a 1, 3 misses = surge on 1-4, etc.). Resets to 0 whenever a surge triggers, and also on a long rest. ## Personality
- **Traits:** Direct to the point of bluntness — has no patience left for people who dance around a question. Watches exits and hands before anything else in a room.
- **Ideals:** Choice. Nobody should have power — or lack it — without some say in the matter. That's what was taken from her, and what she means to make sure isn't taken from anyone else she can help.
- **Bonds:** Sael, who found her first and told her the truth before asking anything in return. The four guardians, briefly connected to at Ravensmoor — proof that whatever she's afraid of becoming, others have crossed that same ground and come out the other side.
- **Flaws:** Years of isolation trained her to expect betrayal or exploitation from anyone offering help. Trusts slowly, and still braces for the offer to turn out conditional.

## Backstory
Nobody in Ravensmoor's fairground district knew her by anything but "the Spark" — a name said half fondly, half nervously, for a young woman whose untrained magic kept surfacing in small, uncontrolled bursts: a fire that started and stopped itself, a wall that rebuilt itself crookedly overnight, a cart that floated gently across a flooded street. No formal training, no guild registration, no family or teacher to explain any of it. She learned early that power without choice in it isn't power at all — it's just a different kind of cage — and spent months living on the edges of the city, alone, afraid of hurting someone by accident again.

Sael found her in an alley near the fairground's edge, approached with the same patient stillness she'd once wished someone had shown her, and told her the truth about her own years of isolation before asking anything in return. That honesty — and later, a direct, unflinching conversation with Rurik about the difference between raw ability and actual power (choice, freedom) — was enough to bring her in. A brief connection to all four sentinel guardians, arranged so she could feel firsthand that others had survived exactly this kind of fear, sealed it. She joined the party at Ravensmoor, chosen freely rather than recruited, on the promise that Urskelde deals in chaos and unpredictability rather than trying to file her power down into something safer and smaller.
